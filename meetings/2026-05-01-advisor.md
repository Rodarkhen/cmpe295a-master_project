## 2) Accomplished During This Meeting

- Delivered the full end-to-end demo to Prof. Bhaskar. Rodrigo ran the ingestion endpoint to push a supplier price update, Parth narrated the DDPG agent decision, Het showed the physical 4-display ESP32 setup update in near real-time, and Aruna walked the advisor through the dashboard including the live SHAP explanation sentence. The demo ran smoothly with a round-trip latency of 2.7 seconds on the first run and 3.0 seconds on the second.
- Prof. Bhaskar expressed strong approval of the demo. He noted the combination of physical hardware, real-time AI decision-making, and human-readable transparency output is a compelling demonstration that would perform well at Expo.
- Reviewed the 7-day simulation quantitative results with Prof. Bhaskar. He confirmed the +19% profit improvement result is honest and defensible, and that the modest difference from the episodic +22% result should be explained in the paper as expected variance from a longer, more realistic scenario rather than being treated as a regression.
- Prof. Bhaskar reviewed Parth Gala's paper draft sections and provided written comments. He asked for the DQN vs. DDPG comparison table to be restructured to make the business-facing interpretation more prominent: emphasize price stability improvements alongside the raw reward numbers.
- Scope expansion discussion initiated by Prof. Bhaskar: After reviewing the demo, the advisor raised the point that the current system is entirely store-facing. The store manager sees the dashboard, the displays update, and the AI makes pricing decisions. However, the end consumer — the shopper — has no visibility into or benefit from the system beyond seeing a price on a shelf. Prof. Bhaskar suggested that expanding the scope to include a shopper-facing interface would significantly increase the project's real-world impact and its appeal at Expo and in the paper.
- Prof. Bhaskar outlined several specific features he would like to see explored for the shopper-facing layer: a price comparison view where shoppers can see how the current price compares to the same product at nearby stores or online; niche or specialty product listings that smaller stores could promote through the platform with an optional premium placement fee, creating a small marketplace dynamic; personalized deal alerts where frequent shoppers could opt in to receive notifications when a product they regularly buy drops below a price threshold; and a transparency feature where shoppers can see a simplified version of why a price changed, building customer trust in AI-driven pricing. He framed these as 295B scope items but asked the team to include a dedicated section in the paper describing the planned shopper-facing layer as future work.
- Prof. Bhaskar confirmed 295A is on track for a satisfactory outcome based on what was demonstrated today. He asked to see a full paper draft by May 15.
