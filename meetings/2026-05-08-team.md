## 2) Accomplished During This Meeting

- Debriefed on Prof. Bhaskar's feedback from the May 1 demo. The team agreed the demo went well and that the advisor's response was more positive than expected. The shopper-facing scope suggestion was the main new input to process.
- Discussed the shopper-facing layer in depth. The team agreed to scope it as a dedicated 295B workstream with the following planned features, listed roughly in priority order:
  1. Price comparison view: for a given product, show the current AI-decided price alongside a reference price (e.g., last week's price, the rule-based baseline price, or a manually entered competitor price). This is the most straightforward to implement given data we already collect.
  2. Niche and specialty product listings: smaller stores or specialty departments could promote products with limited stock through a featured listing slot in the shopper interface, with an optional premium placement fee. This introduces a lightweight marketplace dynamic that does not exist in the current system.
  3. Personalized deal alerts: shoppers can opt into price drop notifications for products they regularly buy. The system already tracks purchase patterns through the demand model; this feature surfaces that data to the shopper.
  4. Shopper-facing price transparency: a simplified version of the SHAP explanation sentence that avoids technical language. For example, "Today's price is lower because we have a lot of stock and want to move it before the weekend."
- Agreed on which features realistically belong in 295B vs. being purely aspirational. Features 1 and 4 (price comparison and simplified transparency) are straightforward extensions of existing work. Features 2 and 3 (niche listings and deal alerts) require new backend services and a shopper identity model that do not currently exist. The team agreed to commit to 1 and 4 for 295B and describe 2 and 3 as future work within the future work section.
- Divided the remaining paper writing tasks and set hard deadlines given the May 15 advisor review target. Each member will have their sections in the shared Google Doc by May 12 so there is time to merge and polish before sending to Prof. Bhaskar.
- Discussed the overall architecture extension needed for the shopper-facing layer. The proposed approach is a lightweight React web app that subscribes to the same MQTT broker (via MQTT-over-WebSocket) and displays live prices and SHAP transparency sentences for each product. This reuses the existing infrastructure without requiring new backend services for the initial version.
